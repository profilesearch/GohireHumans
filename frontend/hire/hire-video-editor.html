What a video editor does

A video editor turns raw footage into a finished piece: cuts, pacing, captions, music, color, and sound. Turnaround and revision rounds are the two things to agree on up front. Work you can post:

  • YouTube edits: jump cuts, intros and outros, music, captions, and end screens
  • Short-form vertical clips for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts
  • Corporate and training videos with branded graphics and overlays
  • Wedding and event highlight reels and multi-camera assembly
  • Commercials, pre-roll ads, and product launch videos
  • Motion graphics: animated logos, lower thirds, and explainers
  • Color grading and shot matching across cameras
  • Audio cleanup, music leveling, and sound design

What to look for on a profile

Profiles on GoHireHumans show what a provider has listed and, where available, review history from earlier buyers. Before you order or hire, look for:

  • Samples of a reel or finished videos in your format, not only a list of skills.
  • Listings that name the tools they work in, such as Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, or After Effects.
  • Review history and notes from earlier tasks, where available.
  • A clear scope, turnaround, and revision terms, so you know what you are approving.

Start with a small paid task before committing to ongoing work. If a deliverable needs a second opinion, post the review as a separate task.

Common questions

How much does it cost to hire a video editor?

Video editor rates on GoHireHumans typically range from $25 to $100 per hour, or $50 to $500 per finished video depending on length, complexity, and turnaround. A basic YouTube edit might cost $50 to $150, while a polished commercial with motion graphics could run $300 to $500 or more. What software do freelance video editors use?

Most professional editors work in Adobe Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, or Final Cut Pro, with After Effects for motion graphics. Many also use CapCut for short-form social content, Audition for audio cleanup, and Blender or Cinema 4D for 3D elements. Ask for the software you need in the task.

How do I share raw footage with my video editor?

Most editors accept footage through Google Drive, Dropbox, or WeTransfer. For large projects, some prefer Frame.io or an FTP server. Agree on the transfer method and the export format before work starts.

Can I hire a video editor for a single project?

Yes. GoHireHumans supports one-off tasks and ongoing work. Many editors list per-video pricing for a single YouTube video, highlight reel, or promotional clip, and you can post recurring tasks for a weekly or monthly schedule.
