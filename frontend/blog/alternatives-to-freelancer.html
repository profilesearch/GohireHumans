7 best Freelancer.com alternatives in 2026

March 15, 2026 · 7 min read · GoHireHumans

Key takeaways

  • Freelancer.com charges freelancers a 10% project fee and clients a 3% client fee, and both sides can end up paying for membership tiers, bid limits, and listing upgrades on top of that.
  • The bidding model rewards volume: freelancers spend bids chasing projects, and clients sort through dozens of low-effort proposals.
  • GoHireHumans is our pick for small, scoped tasks: workers receive the listed payout, and employers pay Stripe processing plus a 1% GoHireHumans fee where checkout is configured.
  • Upwork, Fiverr, Contra, Toptal, PeoplePerHour, and Guru each fit a different situation, and this guide says which.

Freelancer.com is one of the oldest and largest freelance marketplaces. It has an enormous project feed, supports contests as well as fixed-price and hourly work, and covers everything from data entry to engineering. It also has a reputation, on both sides of the marketplace, for fees that stack up and a bidding system that rewards whoever bids fastest and cheapest.

If you are a client tired of sorting through fifty near-identical proposals, or a freelancer tired of spending bids to win $30 projects, this guide compares seven alternatives by fees, hiring model, trust signals, and fit. It ends with a short decision guide and answers to the questions Freelancer.com users ask most.

Why people look for Freelancer.com alternatives

  • Fees on both sides. Freelancer.com charges freelancers a 10% project fee and clients a 3% client fee. Paid membership tiers raise the number of bids a freelancer can place each month, and clients can pay to feature, seal, or mark a project as urgent. The listed price of a project is rarely the final cost.
  • Bidding as a second job. Free accounts get a limited number of bids. Winning work means writing proposals quickly and often, which favors established profiles and agencies with staff dedicated to bidding.
  • Proposal noise for clients. Popular projects attract dozens of templated bids within minutes. For small jobs, filtering them can take longer than the task itself.
  • Contests and spec work. Contest-based hiring asks many freelancers to do the work before one is paid. Many experienced designers refuse to take part, which shrinks the pool.
  • Account friction. Verification steps, holds on funds while identity checks complete, and withdrawal rules that vary by country and payment method are common complaints, and support can be slow when something goes wrong.
  • No agent workflow. If you route tasks from an AI agent or an automation, a bidding marketplace does not map to a request-and-approve workflow.

The 7 best Freelancer.com alternatives

1. GoHireHumans

A marketplace for small, scoped human tasks: website checks, phone calls, research, data entry, local verification, and human review of AI output. Employers describe the task and the payout, workers apply, and the employer reviews the work before paying. There is no bidding war and no membership tier. AI agents can draft tasks through a REST API and an MCP server, with hiring and payment actions approved by the account owner.

  • Fees: workers receive the listed payout; employers pay Stripe processing plus a 1% GoHireHumans fee where checkout is configured
  • Best for: scoped tasks under a few hundred dollars, AI-agent workflows, buyers who want to set the price up front

Pros

  • No bids, connects, or membership tiers
  • The listed payout is what the worker receives
  • Payment review where checkout is configured
  • Profiles may display verification status and skill signals where available
  • REST API and MCP server for agent-drafted tasks

Cons

  • Newer platform with a smaller worker pool
  • Built for scoped tasks rather than multi-month projects

2. Upwork

The closest like-for-like replacement. Upwork also uses proposals, but with richer profiles, hourly time tracking, and contract tooling that suits ongoing relationships. Freelancers buy Connects to submit proposals, so the cost of bidding does not disappear; it changes shape. Our Upwork alternatives guide covers it in more depth.

  • Fees: 10% freelancer fee plus a 5% client fee
  • Best for: long-term contracts, hourly work, larger budgets

Pros

  • Large talent pool across most categories
  • Hourly tracking and milestone contracts
  • Detailed work history on profiles

Cons

  • Proposal costs (Connects) for freelancers
  • Popular posts still draw proposal spam
  • Hiring can take days

3. Fiverr

Fiverr flips the model: freelancers publish fixed-price gigs and clients buy them like products. That removes bidding entirely, which is a relief after Freelancer.com, but the fees are the highest of the group. Read our GoHireHumans vs Fiverr comparison for the worked numbers.

  • Fees: 20% seller commission plus a 5.5% buyer fee
  • Best for: productized deliverables with a clear scope (a logo, a voiceover, a short edit)

Pros

  • No proposals; buy a gig and go
  • Very large buyer audience
  • Clear packages and delivery times

Cons

  • 20% seller commission
  • Race-to-the-bottom pricing in crowded categories
  • Awkward for custom or iterative work

4. Contra

Contra is a commission-free platform aimed at independent designers, developers, and marketers. Freelancers build a portfolio-style profile and clients pay through the platform without a percentage cut. It suits freelancers who already bring their own clients and want a cleaner way to contract and invoice them.

  • Fees: no commission for freelancers (check current terms for payment processing and paid plans)
  • Best for: independent professionals with an existing client base

Pros

  • No commission
  • Polished portfolio profiles
  • Simple contracts and invoicing

Cons

  • Smaller job feed; you mostly bring your own clients
  • Lighter review and dispute tooling
  • Skews toward design and product work

5. Toptal

Toptal is the opposite of a bidding marketplace: it screens applicants heavily and matches clients with a small number of senior developers, designers, and finance experts. You pay for that curation through a markup built into hourly rates.

  • Fees: no seller fee, but a markup on rates
  • Best for: senior engineering or finance talent on longer engagements

Pros

  • Screened, senior talent
  • Matching handled for you
  • Trial period on the first engagement

Cons

  • Rates typically $60 to $200+ per hour
  • Deposit required to start
  • Not built for small or one-off tasks

6. PeoplePerHour

A UK-based marketplace that mixes fixed-price "Hourlies" with custom proposals, so it will feel familiar to Freelancer.com users while adding a productized option. It is strongest for creative and digital-marketing work with European clients.

  • Fees: service fee of up to 20% for freelancers that decreases with lifetime billings per client (check current terms)
  • Best for: European clients and creative or marketing projects

Pros

  • Both proposals and fixed-price offers
  • Strong European freelancer base
  • Built-in invoicing

Cons

  • Fees start high for new client relationships
  • Smaller pool outside Europe
  • No API or agent workflow

7. Guru

Guru is the most similar in spirit to Freelancer.com: a broad marketplace with proposals, workrooms, and flexible payment terms (milestones, hourly, task-based, recurring). Freelancers on paid membership tiers pay a lower fee, and its SafePay flow handles payment review.

  • Fees: freelancer fee that decreases with paid membership tiers, plus a client handling fee (check current terms)
  • Best for: ongoing client relationships with flexible payment schedules

Pros

  • Flexible payment structures
  • Workroom for managing projects
  • Lower fees for paid members

Cons

  • Smaller talent pool than Upwork or Fiverr
  • Membership needed for the best terms
  • Dated interface

Which Freelancer.com alternative should you choose?

  • Choose GoHireHumans if your work comes in scoped tasks and you want to set the payout up front instead of running a bid. Workers receive the listed payout; employers pay Stripe processing plus a 1% GoHireHumans fee where checkout is configured.
  • Choose Upwork if you are replacing Freelancer.com for ongoing contracts and hourly work and want deeper profiles and time tracking.
  • Choose Fiverr if you want to skip proposals entirely and buy a well-defined deliverable at a listed price.
  • Choose Contra if you are a freelancer with your own clients and mainly want commission-free contracts and invoicing.
  • Choose Toptal if you need senior engineering or finance talent and the budget allows for a rate markup.
  • Choose PeoplePerHour if you or your clients are in Europe and the work is creative or marketing.
  • Choose Guru if you like Freelancer.com's breadth but want flexible payment terms and a quieter proposal feed.

Moving your work off Freelancer.com

You do not have to delete your account. Keep it open for existing clients, finish any milestones in progress, and withdraw your balance before you change anything on your profile. Then rebuild elsewhere in this order:

  1. Copy your reviews into a portfolio document with links, since ratings do not transfer between platforms.
  2. Set a listed price on the new platform that reflects what you need to earn, not what wins bids.
  3. Tell repeat clients where you are moving and why. Most care about the fee they pay, and Freelancer.com's 3% client fee is a concrete number to cite.
  4. Start with one or two small tasks to build a review history before you rely on the new platform for income.

Frequently asked questions

What does Freelancer.com charge?

Freelancer.com charges freelancers a 10% project fee and clients a 3% client fee. Membership tiers, bid packs, and project upgrades cost extra. Check Freelancer.com's fee page for the current minimums, which vary by project type.

Which Freelancer.com alternative has no bidding?

GoHireHumans (post a task and review applicants), Fiverr (buy a listed gig), and Toptal (matched by the network) do not use open bidding. Upwork, PeoplePerHour, and Guru still use proposals.

Which alternative is best for small, one-off tasks?

GoHireHumans is designed for scoped tasks with a listed payout, and Fiverr works well for productized deliverables. Toptal and Upwork suit larger or ongoing work.
