Freelancing guide

Where can I offer my services online?

Published March 16, 2026 · GoHireHumans

Last updated: October 26, 2026

You can offer services online through fixed-price marketplaces such as GoHireHumans and Fiverr, proposal marketplaces such as Upwork and Freelancer.com, portfolio platforms such as Contra, or a LinkedIn Service Page. The useful distinction is not which site is universally best: it is whether you want to publish a service buyers can order, apply to a posted brief, or find leads and arrange the work yourself.

Where can I offer my services online?

  • A repeatable remote deliverable: consider a fixed-price listing for spreadsheet cleanup, design, research or written feedback. State exactly what the buyer receives.
  • A project with a specific brief: proposal marketplaces let you respond to the client's requirements. Budget for proposal costs as well as fees on paid work.
  • Professional-network leads: a Service Page or portfolio can introduce your work, but you may need to find the buyer and agree the contract and payment process separately.
  • A screened talent network: Toptal has an application and screening process rather than an open directory where anyone can immediately publish an offer.
  • Local work: state your service area, travel limits and any access or safety requirements. A remote-service listing is not a substitute for local availability.

Where can I post my services for free?

GoHireHumans has free signup and service listings. Contra has a free profile and core workflows; LinkedIn Service Pages are free, with optional paid features. Upwork is free to join and create projects, but proposals and paid contracts can carry costs.

Free to join, free to list and free to transact are different promises. Before investing time, check registration, proposal credits or paid leads, commission, buyer charges, withdrawal minimums and currency conversion. A free listing can receive no orders. Which site fits my service and experience?

GoHireHumans: small, scoped service listings

GoHireHumans is a smaller, newer marketplace for human work and clearly labeled AI services. You can list a fixed-price service or, after finishing free Stripe payout setup, apply to jobs accepting applications. Open jobs are limited right now. Workers receive the listed payout. Employers pay a 1% platform fee plus a fixed 3% processing charge where checkout is configured. GoHireHumans is not an escrow provider or a guarantor. See the worker guide to small paid tasks and payout eligibility guide.

Fiverr: packaged services

Fiverr's Gig format fits an offer you can define in advance. Account creation is free, but seller accounts and Gigs are reviewed and subject to approval. Keep the seller deduction and small-order buyer charge in mind when comparing the full transaction cost.

Upwork and Freelancer.com: respond to project briefs

Choose these models if you want to assess individual briefs and write relevant proposals. Free signup does not make every proposal free. Upwork's Connects requirements vary; Freelancer.com's balance requirement is account funding, not a nonrefundable signup fee. Check a project's scope and the costs before applying.

Contra: portfolio and on-platform projects

Contra combines a portfolio with on-platform payment workflows. Commission-free does not mean every payment or payout is fee-free. It documents payout review and dispute assistance; refunds require mutual agreement, and assistance is not a promise of collection. Review the applicable terms before starting a project.

LinkedIn Service Pages: professional leads

A free Service Page can describe your offering within your professional profile. Use it as a lead channel, not as a managed checkout. Optional paid features are separate from the free page; payment follows the provider's preferred process outside LinkedIn.

Toptal: an application-based network

Toptal describes a screening process for talent. It is a different route from posting an open service listing. Its published matching subscription and client invoicing do not establish a universal worker fee or when talent receives funds.

Can I sell services without my own website?

Yes: a hosted listing can give you a service page to share. Your own website is another option, but it is not a marketplace with built-in buyer demand. You would need to arrange hosting, attract visitors, agree contracts and choose a payment process. Start with a page you can keep accurate rather than assuming that publishing anywhere produces clients.

What should my first service listing include?

Copy this scoped template and replace every placeholder with terms you can actually meet:

Title: I will deliver [specific output] for [type of buyer].
Included: [quantity, format and quality checks].
Not included: [scope limits]; [revision limit] is included.
Delivery time: [turnaround after receiving all buyer inputs].
Price: [fixed price for this scope].
What I need from you: [files, source links, instructions and access permissions].
Work sample: [a genuine sample you have permission to share].

Complete required profile fields so buyers understand your offer. Set a sustainable price, protect private information and share the link only where promotion is allowed. Completing a profile does not guarantee rankings or a first-client timeline.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I post my services online for free?

GoHireHumans offers free service listings. Contra offers a free profile and core freelance workflows, and LinkedIn Service Pages are free with optional paid features. Free publishing is different from free transactions: check proposal, payment, payout and client charges before choosing a platform.

Can I sell services without my own website?

Yes. A hosted service listing or professional Service Page can explain your offer and provide a link to share. You still need a clear scope, a genuine work sample and a way to reach suitable buyers. Publishing a page does not guarantee views or orders.

Which service platform should a beginner choose?

Choose by the work you can demonstrate, the platform requirements and the full cost of finding and completing a project. A fixed-price listing suits a repeatable deliverable; proposal marketplaces suit responding to a specific brief. Neither model guarantees acceptance, clients or earnings.
