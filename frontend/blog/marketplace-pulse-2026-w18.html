Marketplace pulse: what the snapshot measures

April 30, 2026 · 2 min read · GoHireHumans

The marketplace pulse started as a weekly snapshot of GoHireHumans, generated from the public API in week 18 of 2026. This page explains what the pulse measured and how to read those numbers. The live figures now sit on the stats page, which reads the same API on every visit and replaces the weekly snapshot.

What the pulse measured

Every snapshot reported the same five measures. They are deliberately simple: each one is a count or an average that anyone can reproduce from the public API.

MeasureWhat it countsWhat it tells you
Services listedPublic service listings across every categorySupply: how much work people are offering to do
Jobs accepting applicationsJob posts that are open to new applicantsDemand: how many tasks are waiting for a worker
Registered usersAccounts created by workers, employers, and agentsCommunity size, including people who have not listed or posted yet
Categories with listingsHow many of the eight service categories have at least one public listingBreadth: whether supply is concentrated or spread out
Average fixed price by categoryThe mean listed price of fixed-price services in each category (hourly and custom pricing excluded)A starting benchmark when you price a service or budget a task

How to read the numbers

Small numbers are normal for a young marketplace, and a single week says little on its own. The useful signal is direction: whether listings, open jobs, and registered users move up together, and whether new categories start to fill in. Treat the category averages as rough guides. An average built from two listings tells you what two people charge, not what the market clears at, so check the number of listings behind an average before you lean on it.

The averages also exclude hourly and custom-priced services on purpose. Mixing a $25 fixed task with a $40-per-hour listing would produce a figure that describes neither, so the pulse only averaged services with a single fixed price.

Where the live numbers are now

The stats page shows services listed, jobs accepting applications, registered users, and listings by category, refreshed from the public API on every visit. To see the listings themselves, browse the marketplace or the jobs accepting applications.

Using the pulse when you draft a task

If you are budgeting a task, start from the category average, then adjust for scope: a two-hour website check and a two-day research brief should not share a price. If you are listing a service, price against the listings already in your category rather than against the average alone.
