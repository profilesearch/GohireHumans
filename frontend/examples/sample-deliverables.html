Illustrative task reports

Know what a good human-task deliverable looks like.

Use these examples to scope small tasks that are easy to verify, accept, and repeat.

Sample deliverables

These smaller illustrative examples are not the full starter offers or completed customer orders. Each has its own suggested budget and limited scope. Provider availability and turnaround are not guaranteed. Agree scope, price, timing, and acceptance criteria before work. Opening a draft does not publish, book, or charge anything. Never put credentials or sensitive customer data in a public brief.

Website QA · $25 suggested

Sample website QA report

  • Flow tested: homepage → signup → email confirmation → dashboard.
  • Result: signup completed, but confirmation copy did not explain next step.
  • Issues found: mobile hero CTA wraps awkwardly; password error message unclear; dashboard first screen has no checklist.
  • Recommended fixes: shorten CTA, add password rules before submit, add first-action checklist.

Inputs: one safe sandbox flow of up to 4 steps, one agreed browser/device, expected behavior, and a test checklist. Exclusions: production submissions, purchases, live messages, security/load testing, code fixes, and cross-device coverage.

Deliverable: a step checklist, issue notes, and screenshots where safe and available. Acceptance criteria: cover each agreed step with expected/actual results and flag blocked steps; fixes and retests need a separate agreement.

Timing: agree delivery after provider acceptance and complete, safe inputs; availability and turnaround are not guaranteed.

Phone check · $25 suggested

Sample phone verification report

  • Called location at 2:10pm local time.
  • Confirmed hours and availability.
  • Captured exact answer and any uncertainty.
  • If unreachable, report no-answer or refusal rather than assuming confirmation.

Inputs: one business, its public phone number, up to 2 questions, and an agreed calling window; up to 2 call attempts. Exclusions: local visits, purchases, paid access, impersonation, covert recording, sensitive data, and guaranteed contact or confirmation.

Deliverable: a timestamped call log with answers or no-answer/refusal outcomes and uncertainty notes. Acceptance criteria: document both questions and each attempted call; an honestly recorded unreachable outcome is valid, not a confirmed fact.

Timing: agree delivery after provider acceptance and complete, safe inputs; availability and turnaround are not guaranteed.
